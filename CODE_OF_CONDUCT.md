# MooNsEvents Code of Conduct

MooNsEvents is built by people with different backgrounds, experience levels, cultures, and
perspectives. We want participation to be useful, respectful, and safe for everyone.

**[Overview](README.md)** · **[Contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md)** ·
**[Support](SUPPORT.md)** · **[Governance](GOVERNANCE.md)** ·
**[Security](SECURITY.md)**

## Expected conduct

Everyone participating in MooNsEvents is expected to:

- Be respectful when reviewing code, discussing product decisions, or disagreeing with an idea.
- Critique the work and its impact, not the person who created it.
- Give actionable feedback with enough context for another contributor to respond.
- Welcome beginners and explain project-specific expectations without gatekeeping.
- Respect privacy. Never expose client data, credentials, private messages, recordings, or
  another person's personal information.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest when proposing providers, services, or commercial
  relationships.
- Accept responsibility, apologize when appropriate, and help repair the effect of mistakes.

## Unacceptable conduct

The following behavior is not accepted:

- Harassment, intimidation, threats, stalking, or sustained disruption.
- Discriminatory, sexualized, insulting, or demeaning language or imagery.
- Personal attacks, deliberate misrepresentation, or publishing private communications.
- Sharing another person's private information without explicit permission.
- Spam, undisclosed promotion, deceptive contributions, or attempts to insert malicious code.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith.

## Scope

This Code applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, Discussions, community spaces,
project events, and private communication related to MooNsEvents. It also applies when someone
publicly represents the project or acts in an official project role.

## Reporting a concern

Do not post sensitive conduct reports or personal information in a public issue.

Use the repository's
[private vulnerability reporting form](https://github.com/schowdary75/moonsevents/security/advisories/new)
and begin the title with `[Conduct]`. This gives the maintainers a private place to review the
report. Include:

- What happened and where it happened.
- Relevant links, dates, and screenshots.
- Whether the behavior is continuing.
- Any immediate safety or privacy concern.

GitHub-platform abuse can also be reported through GitHub's own **Report content** option.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will review reports privately, consider context and impact, and avoid unnecessary
disclosure. Depending on severity and history, responses may include:

1. A private clarification or request to correct behavior.
2. A formal warning and required corrective action.
3. Temporary limits on repository or community participation.
4. Removal of content, closure of a contribution, or a permanent ban.
5. Escalation to GitHub or relevant authorities when required for safety or law.

Maintainers who are directly involved in a report must not make the final enforcement decision
alone. Good-faith reporters and participants will not be punished for raising a concern.

## Maintainer responsibility

Maintainers are responsible for applying this Code consistently and may edit, reject, or remove
comments, commits, issues, pull requests, or other contributions that violate it.

Questions about ordinary project use belong in [Support](SUPPORT.md), while security
vulnerabilities follow the [Security Policy](SECURITY.md).
